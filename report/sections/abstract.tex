\begin{abstract}
Grounded drug question answering requires both retrieving the appropriate evidence and avoiding unsupported answers, yet Vietnamese lacks dedicated resources for evaluating these capabilities. We construct a Vietnamese drug-information corpus from the national drug formulary and package leaflets, together with a 10{,}000-question retrieval benchmark whose answer locations are known by construction and a 500-item end-to-end evaluation set covering single-turn, multi-turn, unanswerable, out-of-scope, and prompt-injection requests. On the retrieval benchmark, hybrid retrieval followed by reranking performs best and places a correct passage in the top ten for 97.5\% of questions. On top of this retriever, we design an agentic retrieval-augmented generation pipeline that keeps its control flow in code and asks the language model only for small structured decisions, so that it runs on low-cost language models. On multi-turn follow-up questions, single-step RAG leaves 96\% unanswered, whereas the agentic pipeline answers all of them with a nugget recall of 0.91. Over the whole evaluation set, it reduces unanswered questions from 11.9\% to 4.4\% and rejects every unanswerable and prompt-injection request. These results suggest that, once retrieval is strong, agentic steps pay off mainly in conversational settings, and that reliable drug question answering in Vietnamese is achievable with low-cost language models.
