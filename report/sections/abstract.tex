\begin{abstract}
Grounded drug question answering requires both retrieving the appropriate evidence and avoiding unsupported answers, yet no dedicated resource exists for evaluating these capabilities in Vietnamese. We construct a Vietnamese drug-information corpus from the national drug formulary and package leaflets, together with a 10{,}000-question retrieval benchmark whose answer locations are known by construction and a 500-item end-to-end evaluation set covering single-turn, multi-turn, unanswerable, out-of-scope, and prompt-injection requests. On this benchmark, hybrid retrieval with a self-hosted reranker places a correct passage in the top ten for 97.5\% of questions. On top of this retriever, we design an agentic retrieval-augmented generation architecture for low-cost language models that keeps the control flow in deterministic code and limits every model call to one small structured decision. With the commercial Gemini 3.8 Flash, it reaches a truthfulness of 0.923 and covers 94.6\% of the reference facts, substantially outperforming the same model without retrieval, and it reduces hallucinated answers eighteenfold. The open-weight Qwen3.5-9B benefits even more from retrieval, with twenty-five times fewer hallucinated answers, and within the same pipeline it closely matches the commercial model. Beyond providing the first dedicated benchmark for Vietnamese drug-information retrieval, our results reveal that grounding in the corpus accounts for most of the accuracy gain, that query rephrasing gives the agent a clear advantage over one-step RAG on multi-turn conversational follow-ups, and that an iterative judge--refine loop further improves answer truthfulness and completeness by verifying evidence sufficiency.
