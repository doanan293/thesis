\begin{abstract}
Grounded drug question answering requires both retrieving the appropriate evidence and avoiding unsupported answers, yet Vietnamese lacks dedicated resources for evaluating these capabilities. We construct a Vietnamese drug-information corpus from the national drug formulary and package leaflets, together with a 10{,}000-question retrieval benchmark whose answer locations are known by construction and a 500-item end-to-end evaluation set covering single-turn, multi-turn, unanswerable, out-of-scope, and prompt-injection requests. On this benchmark, hybrid retrieval followed by reranking places a correct passage in the top ten for 97.7\% of questions. On top of this retriever, we design an agentic retrieval-augmented generation architecture that maximises answer accuracy and suppresses hallucination. Compared with the same language models answering without retrieval, it reduces hallucinated answers twelvefold for Gemini 3.8 Flash and eightfold for the open-weight Qwen3.5-9B, and it lets the 9-billion-parameter model nearly triple the truthfulness of Gemini answering alone while approaching Gemini with the same architecture. Beyond providing the first dedicated benchmark for Vietnamese drug-information retrieval, our results reveal that the harness and the data, rather than model scale, carry most of the accuracy, and that the agentic gain comes almost entirely from a single query-rephrasing step, which answers the conversational follow-up questions that single-step RAG leaves 96\% unanswered, whereas an iterative judge--refine loop adds cost without a measurable gain.
