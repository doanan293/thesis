\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper built a Vietnamese drug-information corpus, a 10{,}000-question retrieval benchmark and an agentic RAG pipeline. Grounding is what makes the answers usable: it cuts hallucinated answers eighteenfold with Gemini and twenty-five-fold with a 9-billion-parameter open-weight model, which then reaches a truthfulness of 0.913 against 0.923 for Gemini. Query rephrasing makes conversational follow-up questions answerable, whereas the judge--refine loop added cost without a measurable gain, so a small open-weight model with a self-hosted retriever is a viable deployment.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. The benchmark questions are generated by rules and are more regular than patient questions, and the fusion constant was selected on the same benchmark. The golden set was written with one pharmacist, the blind grades come from a single pharmacist, and the judge shares a provider with the Gemini answer model. A cheaper remedy for follow-up questions, such as appending previous turns to the search query, was not tested. Future work should have the answers graded by a larger team of professional pharmacists, and should add questions from real users and a check for answers that mix information about different drugs.
