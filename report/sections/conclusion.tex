\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper built a Vietnamese drug-information corpus, a 10{,}000-question retrieval benchmark with known answer locations, and an agentic RAG pipeline in which every model call is a single small decision. Hybrid fusion followed by reranking gives the best ranking quality, at 97.7\% Hit@10 with an 8B reranker and within one point of it with a 4B reranker at 2.7 times the throughput. End to end, grounding is what makes the answers usable: the pipeline cuts hallucinated answers from 18.3\% to 1.5\% with a hosted model and from 30.6\% to 3.7\% with a 9-billion-parameter open-weight model. Query rephrasing lets the pipeline answer every conversational follow-up question, of which single-step RAG leaves 96\% unanswered, whereas the judge--refine loop added cost without a measurable gain. A small open-weight model with a self-hosted retriever is therefore a viable deployment when the budget excludes hosted models.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. The benchmark questions are generated from the corpus by rules, so they are more regular than questions asked by patients, and the fusion constant and the reranker instruction were selected on the same benchmark. The reference answers of the golden set were written by a language model without pharmacist review, 57 of its items were revised after the first evaluation with criteria that depend only on the item, and the automatic judge shares a provider with the hosted answer model. Calibration measures this risk but cannot remove it. The leaflets come from a single online source.

Future work should add pharmacist review of a subset, questions from real users, and a check for answers that mix information about different drugs.
