\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents a Vietnamese drug-information corpus, a 10{,}000-question retrieval benchmark and an agentic RAG pipeline for low-cost language models. On the benchmark, hybrid retrieval with the self-hosted Qwen3-Reranker-4B places a relevant chunk in the top ten for 97.5\% of the questions, and the self-hosted Qwen3-Embedding-4B outperforms OpenAI text-embedding-3-large. Grounding is what makes the answers usable. It cuts hallucinated answers eighteenfold with Gemini 3.8 Flash and twenty-five-fold with the open-weight Qwen3.5-9B, which then reaches a truthfulness of 0.913 against 0.923 for Gemini 3.8 Flash. Query rephrasing makes conversational follow-up questions answerable, and the judge--refine loop further improves truthfulness and recall by catching incomplete retrieval, confirming that small open-weight models and a self-hosted retriever can support a viable deployment.

Several limitations qualify these conclusions. The benchmark questions are generated by rules and are more regular than patient questions, and the fusion constant is selected on the same benchmark. The golden set is written with one pharmacist, the blind grades come from a single pharmacist, and the judge shares a provider with the Gemini answer model. A cheaper remedy for follow-up questions, such as appending previous turns to the search query, remains untested. Future work should involve several pharmacists in grading the answers and should add real user questions and a check for answers that mix information about different drugs.
