\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

People increasingly ask chatbots about the medicines they take, and a wrong dose or a missed contraindication can cause real harm. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) reduces this risk by grounding answers in authoritative text~\cite{lewis2020rag}, and agentic variants add query rewriting, evidence checks and iterative search~\cite{asai2024selfrag,yan2024crag,jeong2024adaptiverag}. Deploying such a system for Vietnamese drug information raises three practical problems. There is no Vietnamese corpus and benchmark of drug labelling to measure retrieval on; retrieval choices are usually ranked by quality alone, although vector size and reranking throughput decide what can be served; and agentic pipelines are typically built on strong LLMs, while many deployments can only afford small, inexpensive models.

This paper studies these problems on a corpus built from the Vietnamese National Drug Formulary and 2{,}406 drug package leaflets, and asks three research questions. \textbf{RQ1:} Which retrieval configuration gives the best quality for its cost on Vietnamese drug questions? \textbf{RQ2:} Does an agentic pipeline run by low-cost LLMs answer more accurately than one-step RAG with the same retriever, and which steps contribute? \textbf{RQ3:} Does the pipeline refuse unanswerable, off-topic and adversarial requests reliably?
