\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Question answering over drug information supports patients who self-medicate, caregivers who manage prescriptions and pharmacists who counsel customers. Because an incorrect dosage or an omitted contraindication can cause direct harm, answers in this domain must be accurate and traceable to authoritative sources. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) meets this requirement by grounding a language model in retrieved documents~\cite{lewis2020rag}. This paper focuses on a specific setting of this problem: answering Vietnamese questions from official drug labelling when only low-cost language models can be afforded at inference time.

This setting is challenging for three reasons. First, Vietnamese lacks an evaluation resource for drug information. Existing Vietnamese medical datasets address general health questions, intent detection or reading comprehension~\cite{huy2021vimq,nguyen2022spbertqa,luu2021vicoqa}, so retrieval methods cannot be compared on drug labelling, where questions often use brand names, colloquial product names or text without diacritics. Second, retrieval approaches are usually ranked by effectiveness alone~\cite{thakur2021beir,muennighoff2023mteb}. For a deployed system, however, the size of the embedding and the throughput of the reranker matter as much as ranking quality, and it is unclear which combination of sparse, dense, hybrid and reranked retrieval offers the best trade-off. Finally, agentic RAG methods either fine-tune the generator or rely on a capable model to decide when and how to retrieve~\cite{asai2024selfrag,yan2024crag,jeong2024adaptiverag}. Delegating these decisions to small models is risky, and such pipelines are typically reported with a single aggregate score, which reveals neither the contribution of each step nor the behaviour on unanswerable or adversarial requests.

To address these challenges, we first construct a Vietnamese drug-information corpus from the national drug formulary and package leaflets, together with a retrieval benchmark whose answer locations are known by construction. We then use this benchmark to compare retrieval approaches under a common implementation, reporting their cost next to their effectiveness. Finally, we design an agentic RAG pipeline for low-cost models. Our key design choice is to keep the control flow in deterministic code and to restrict every model call to a single, small structured decision, such as whether a request is safe or whether the retrieved evidence is sufficient. In contrast to pipelines that delegate planning to the model, this design only asks the model to perform narrow tasks, which are better suited to small models. It also enforces budgets and loop limits independently of the model, and it allows each step to be removed in controlled ablations.

The study is organised around three research questions. \textbf{RQ1} asks which retrieval approach offers the best trade-off between effectiveness and cost. \textbf{RQ2} asks whether the agentic pipeline answers more accurately than single-step RAG and which of its steps contribute. \textbf{RQ3} asks whether the pipeline reliably declines unanswerable, out-of-scope and adversarial requests.

We evaluate retrieval on 10{,}000 benchmark questions and the complete pipeline on a 500-item golden set that also contains multi-turn, unanswerable, out-of-scope and prompt-injection items. The end-to-end protocol combines established correctness, attribution and safety metrics with paired significance tests and a calibration of the automatic judge. In summary, this paper makes the following contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item A Vietnamese drug-information corpus and a 10{,}000-question retrieval benchmark with known answer locations.
\item A systematic comparison of sparse, dense, hybrid and reranked retrieval that reports effectiveness together with deployment cost.
\item An agentic RAG pipeline designed for low-cost models, together with an end-to-end evaluation protocol with ablations, safety cases and judge calibration that quantifies its benefits and limits.
\end{itemize}
