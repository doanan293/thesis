\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Patients and caregivers increasingly turn to conversational assistants for information about the medicines they use. In this setting an incorrect dosage or an omitted contraindication can cause direct harm, so answers must be accurate and traceable to authoritative sources. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) addresses this requirement by grounding a language model in retrieved documents~\cite{lewis2020rag}, and recent agentic variants further decide when to retrieve, reformulate queries and verify the evidence before answering~\cite{asai2024selfrag,yan2024crag,jeong2024adaptiverag}.

Bringing these techniques to Vietnamese drug information remains difficult for several reasons. Public Vietnamese medical resources concern general health questions rather than drug labelling, so there is no benchmark on which retrieval methods for this domain can be compared. Retrieval studies commonly rank methods by effectiveness alone, although the cost of indexing and reranking largely determines what can be deployed. Agentic pipelines are usually designed around capable, expensive language models, whereas many practical deployments must rely on small, low-cost ones. Finally, such pipelines are rarely evaluated end to end with controlled ablations, safety cases and a validated automatic judge.

This paper investigates evidence-grounded question answering over Vietnamese drug information under a limited inference budget. We address three research questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item[] \textbf{RQ1.} Which retrieval approach offers the best trade-off between effectiveness and cost for Vietnamese drug questions?
\item[] \textbf{RQ2.} Does an agentic RAG pipeline driven by low-cost language models answer more accurately than single-step RAG, and which of its components contribute?
\item[] \textbf{RQ3.} Does such a pipeline reliably decline unanswerable, out-of-scope and adversarial requests?
\end{itemize}

\noindent The contributions of this work are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item A Vietnamese drug-information corpus built from the national drug formulary and package leaflets, together with a large retrieval benchmark whose answer locations are known.
\item A systematic comparison of sparse, dense, hybrid and reranked retrieval on this benchmark, reporting effectiveness alongside deployment cost.
\item An end-to-end evaluation protocol that combines established correctness, attribution and safety metrics with paired ablations and judge calibration, applied to an agentic RAG pipeline designed for low-cost models. \pending{one-sentence headline finding}
\end{itemize}
