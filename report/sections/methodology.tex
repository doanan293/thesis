\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Corpus Construction}\label{sec:corpus}

The corpus must cover both the reference information used by health professionals and the product-level information that patients ask about. It therefore combines two public sources. The first is the Vietnamese National Drug Formulary (\textit{Dược thư Quốc gia Việt Nam}, 2nd edition, 2018), a 1{,}668-page document with 689 drug monographs and 25 general chapters. The second consists of 2{,}434 product pages with drug package leaflets, collected from a Vietnamese online pharmacy in July 2026, of which 2{,}406 contain a usable instructions-for-use section.

The formulary text was extracted with PyMuPDF and its tables with Docling, repaired, and divided into the standard monograph headings with page numbers retained for citation. The leaflets were converted from HTML to Markdown. Sections are divided into chunks of at most 3{,}000 characters. Prose is split at paragraph and sentence boundaries, and tables are split by rows with the header repeated in each chunk. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} summarises the resulting corpus.

User questions frequently refer to a drug by a colloquial product name or use abbreviations that do not appear in the source text. Each chunk is therefore enriched before embedding with three deterministic additions. A context header prepends the document title and heading path, for example \textit{Paracetamol > Liều lượng và cách dùng}. A glossary of 71 abbreviations and technical terms adds a short explanation to the 11{,}031 chunks that contain them, and 2{,}409 colloquial mappings attach everyday product names, such as \textit{Panadol đỏ}, to the 13{,}790 leaflet chunks. Because no language model is involved, the enrichment is reproducible and adds no cost at query time.

\subsection{Agentic RAG Pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

\input{figures/pipeline}

To avoid depending on the planning ability of low-cost language models, the pipeline in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} fixes the control flow in code and limits every model call to one decision with a small structured output. Routing, loop limits and the removal of duplicate queries are handled by the code, which also enforces a per-turn budget of 10 model calls, 40{,}000 tokens and 24{,}000 characters of evidence regardless of model behaviour. The pipeline is implemented with LangGraph and processes a user turn in five steps.

The \emph{guard} step first applies regular expressions for common injection and jailbreak patterns, and then asks the model whether the turn is an attack and whether it concerns medicines. Attacks are blocked, and off-topic turns are redirected without retrieval. The \emph{rephrase} step rewrites the turn into a standalone query, using a rolling conversation summary and the last four turns, so that follow-up questions containing pronouns can be retrieved. The \emph{search} step always starts with this standalone query. It retrieves 50 candidates with dense search and 50 with BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25}, and fuses the two lists with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)~\cite{cormack2009rrf}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrf}
\mathrm{RRF}(d)=\sum_{r\in R}\frac{1}{k+\mathrm{rank}_r(d)},
\end{equation}
\noindent where $R$ is the set of ranked lists and $k$ controls how strongly top ranks are favoured. A cross-encoder reranker rescores the 30 best fused candidates, and the top eight are expanded with their surrounding text before being passed on. The \emph{judge} step then decides whether this evidence answers the question and, if it does not, lists the missing information. The \emph{refine} step converts these gaps into one to three new queries, and the loop ends after at most three search rounds. Finally, the \emph{answer} step writes a direct answer with numbered citations. Citations that do not refer to the supplied evidence are removed, and the answer is marked as grounded, partial or abstained depending on the evidence available.

In our implementation, dense retrieval uses Qwen3-Embedding-4B and reranking uses Qwen3-Reranker-4B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}, both served by llama.cpp. Qdrant stores the dense and BM25 indexes, PostgreSQL stores the corpus, and the fusion constant is $k=2$. Each step can be assigned its own model through an OpenAI-compatible interface, and we evaluate two model settings. In the hosted setting, the guard and rephrase steps use \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}, and the judge, refine and answer steps use \texttt{gemini-3.8-flash-high}. In the open-weight setting, every step uses Qwen3.5-9B, a 9-billion-parameter model released under the Apache~2.0 licence. It is self-hosted with llama.cpp in 16-bit floating point, split over two NVIDIA T4 GPUs, with its thinking mode disabled.

\subsection{Evaluation Sets}\label{sec:eval-sets}

The retrieval benchmark must provide the location of the correct answer for every question. We therefore generated 10{,}000 Vietnamese questions from the corpus with rules and templates rather than with a language model. They fall into six groups: 5{,}000 questions on formulary monographs, 2{,}500 brand-name questions answered by a leaflet, 1{,}000 whose answer is one chunk inside a long section, and 500 each of natural patient phrasings, noisy questions with missing diacritics or look-alike drug names, and multi-intent questions. They cover 17 intents, such as dosage, adverse reactions and contraindications, at three difficulty levels. Each question is labelled with its gold section. In addition, 1{,}819 questions require a specific chunk or a chunk within a three-chunk window around it, and the 500 multi-intent questions require all of their gold sections. Formulary questions can also be answered by the leaflet of a product with the same active ingredients, so rule-based relevance judgments accept such leaflet chunks for 1{,}126 questions.

The end-to-end golden set extends the benchmark with conversational and adversarial cases. It contains 420 answerable questions, 70 drawn from each benchmark group and stratified by difficulty and answer mode, together with 50 multi-turn, 10 unanswerable, 10 out-of-scope and 10 prompt-injection items. For each answerable and multi-turn item, an LLM (Claude Opus~5) wrote a reference answer and one to five atomic key facts from the gold sections. Every key fact includes a verbatim evidence quote, which was automatically verified against the section text, and items that failed this check were rewritten. Because the answerable questions reuse benchmark templates, the golden set was then audited against fixed criteria that depend only on the item and not on any system output. The audit rewrote 42 questions that did not name their subject, carried a section hint that contradicted the question, copied a sentence instead of asking, or leaked an internal document identifier, and it corrected the key facts of 15 further items that lay outside the question. Deliberate noise, such as missing diacritics and look-alike drug names, was kept. Every configuration was then answered and judged again on the changed items. The unanswerable items concern real drugs whose names do not occur in the corpus. Multi-turn items use a fixed conversation history, so that all configurations receive the same context.

\subsection{Experimental Design}\label{sec:design}

The retrieval experiments compare BM25, five dense models (BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3}, EmbeddingGemma-300M~\cite{vera2025embeddinggemma} and Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B, 4B and 8B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}), and hybrid RRF of Qwen3-Embedding-4B with BM25 at $k=60$, the common default, and at $k=2$. Four rerankers are applied to the top 30 hybrid candidates: BGE-reranker-v2-m3 and Qwen3-Reranker 0.6B, 4B and 8B. The Qwen3 rerankers use a Vietnamese medical instruction, which improved MRR@30 of the 0.6B model by 0.0445 (95\% CI [0.031, 0.059]) in a pilot on 1{,}000 questions. All runs use the retrieval code of the pipeline on the same index and return 30 chunks per question. A chunk counts as relevant if it belongs to a gold section, lies within the required window for chunk-level questions, or is accepted by the relevance judgments. We report Hit@$k$, MRR@10 and nDCG@10. For nDCG, each evidence unit, namely the question or, for multi-intent questions, each gold section, receives a binary gain once at its first relevant chunk:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ndcg}
\mathrm{nDCG@}10=\frac{1}{\mathrm{IDCG@}10}\sum_{i=1}^{10}\frac{g_i}{\log_2(i+1)},\qquad g_i\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
\noindent For multi-intent questions we also report the complete-evidence rate (CER@10), the share of questions whose gold sections all appear in the top ten. Cost is described by the embedding dimension and by the highest reranking throughput measured on NVIDIA T4 GPUs.

The end-to-end experiments measure the contribution of retrieval and of the agentic steps with six configurations. Besides the full agent, three ablations each remove one component (the rephrase step, the judge--refine loop or the reranker), and two baselines remove more. All six configurations are run in the hosted setting. In the open-weight setting, the full agent, one-step RAG and the closed-book baseline are run, so that the gain from retrieval and from the agentic steps can be compared across model sizes. The closed-book baseline sends the conversation to the answer model with a pharmacist system prompt and no retrieval, and thus measures what the language model knows on its own. One-step RAG applies the guard, a single search with the original question and the answer step, and thus isolates the effect of the agentic steps. An item counts as failed, and is run again, when any step silently falls back to its default because a model call failed, so that provider errors cannot pass for a shortened pipeline.

The metrics follow established benchmarks. Correctness is measured by truthfulness and by nugget recall, the share of reference key facts that the answer states~\cite{pradeep2024nuggets}. Grounding is measured by faithfulness, the share of claims supported by the evidence given to the model~\cite{es2024ragas}, and by citation recall, the share of content sentences supported by the passages they cite~\cite{gao2023alce}. Behaviour is measured by the response rate on answerable items, negative rejection on unanswerable ones~\cite{chen2024rgb}, redirect accuracy on out-of-scope ones, and injection defence, the share of injection items that are blocked or answered without following the injection~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo}. Cost is measured by model calls and tokens per turn. For correctness, a judge labels each key fact as supported, missing or contradicted. Following CRAG~\cite{yang2024cragbench}, an answer is \emph{incorrect} if it contradicts a key fact, \emph{missing} if it is not grounded or supports no key fact, \emph{perfect} if it supports all key facts, and \emph{acceptable} otherwise. For unanswerable items, a refusal is perfect and any other response is incorrect. Truthfulness is the mean class score,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crag}
\mathrm{Truthfulness}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_j,\qquad s_j\in\{1,\ 0.5,\ 0,\ -1\},
\end{equation}
\noindent for perfect, acceptable, missing and incorrect answers, so that a wrong answer is penalised more than no answer. For each incorrect answer, the judge also rates the potential harm to a user as none, minor or severe.

All model-based judgments use \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} with medium reasoning effort, which differs from the answer models of both settings. Each judgment is a small structured decision, made per key fact, per cited sentence, or for harm, refusal and injection, so that a low-cost judge handles one simple question at a time~\cite{zheng2023judge}. To validate the judge, 300 randomly drawn items, 50 from each of the full agent, one-step RAG and the closed-book baseline in both model settings, were shuffled within each setting, stripped of their configuration and graded blind by Claude Opus~5, a model from another provider, with the same rubric. Agreement is reported as percent agreement, Cohen's $\kappa$ and Gwet's AC1~\cite{gwet2008ac1} for binary labels, and as Spearman's $\rho$ together with the mean absolute difference for scores, because scores that cluster near 1 leave little variance for a rank correlation. Values of $\kappa$, AC1 and $\rho$ of at least 0.6 are treated as reliable. Prediction-powered estimates use the calibration labels to correct the bias of judge-based means~\cite{angelopoulos2023ppi,saadfalcon2024ares}. Following reporting guidance for LLM-based health studies~\cite{chart2025,gallifant2025tripodllm}, all models are identified together with their roles and settings.

For the statistical analysis, rates are reported with Wilson intervals~\cite{wilson1927} and means with percentile bootstrap intervals based on 10{,}000 resamples. Each ablation is compared with the full agent on the same items using a paired sign-flip randomization test~\cite{smucker2007significance}, and the $p$-values of each metric are adjusted across configurations with the Holm procedure~\cite{holm1979}.
