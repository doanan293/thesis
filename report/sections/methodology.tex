\subsection{Corpus Construction}\label{sec:corpus}

The corpus must cover both the reference information used by health professionals and the product-level information that patients ask about. It therefore combines two public sources. The first is the Vietnamese National Drug Formulary (\textit{Dược thư Quốc gia Việt Nam}, 2nd edition, 2018), with 689 drug monographs and 25 general chapters. The second consists of 2{,}406 drug package leaflets collected from a Vietnamese online pharmacy in July 2026.

The formulary is converted from PDF to text and tables and divided into the standard monograph headings, with page numbers kept for citation. The leaflets are converted from HTML to Markdown. Sections are divided into chunks of at most 3{,}000 characters. Prose is split at paragraph and sentence boundaries, and tables are split by rows with the header repeated in each chunk. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} summarises the resulting corpus.

User questions frequently refer to a drug by a colloquial product name or use abbreviations that do not appear in the source text. Each chunk is therefore enriched before embedding with three deterministic additions. We prepend a context header with the document title and heading path, for example \textit{Paracetamol > Liều lượng và cách dùng}. A glossary of 71 abbreviations and technical terms adds a short explanation to the 11{,}031 chunks that contain them, and 2{,}409 colloquial mappings attach everyday product names, such as \textit{Panadol đỏ}, to the 13{,}790 leaflet chunks. No language model is involved, so the enrichment is reproducible and adds no language-model calls at query time.

\subsection{Agentic RAG Pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

\input{figures/pipeline}

To avoid depending on the planning ability of low-cost language models, the pipeline in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} fixes the control flow in code and limits every model call to one decision with a small structured output. Routing, loop limits and duplicate-query removal are handled by the code, which also enforces a per-turn budget of 10 model calls, 40{,}000 tokens and 24{,}000 characters of evidence.

The \emph{guard} step first applies regular expressions for common injection and jailbreak patterns, and then asks the model whether the turn is an attack and whether it concerns medicines. Attacks are blocked, off-topic turns are redirected, and small talk is answered without retrieval. The \emph{rephrase} step rewrites the turn into a standalone query, using a rolling conversation summary and the last four turns, so that evidence can be retrieved for follow-up questions that refer back with pronouns. The \emph{search} step always starts with this standalone query. It retrieves 50 candidates with dense search and 50 with BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25}, and fuses the two lists with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)~\cite{cormack2009rrf}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrf}
\mathrm{RRF}(d)=\sum_{r\in R}\frac{1}{k+\mathrm{rank}_r(d)},
\end{equation}
\noindent where $R$ is the set of ranked lists and a small constant $k$ favours the top ranks of each list. A cross-encoder reranker rescores the 30 best fused candidates, and the top five are expanded with their surrounding text before being passed on. The \emph{judge} step then decides whether this evidence answers the question and, if it does not, lists the missing information. The \emph{refine} step converts these gaps into one to three new queries, and the loop ends after at most three search rounds. Finally, the \emph{answer} step writes a direct answer with numbered citations. It receives both the original question and the rewritten query, because the rewrite can drop details of a self-contained question. Citations that do not refer to the supplied evidence are removed, and each answer is labelled as grounded, partial or an abstention depending on the evidence available.

In our implementation, dense retrieval uses Qwen3-Embedding-4B and reranking uses Qwen3-Reranker-4B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}, both served by llama.cpp. The pipeline is implemented with LangGraph, Qdrant holds the dense and BM25 indexes, and the fusion constant is $k=2$. Each step can be assigned its own model, and we evaluate two model settings. In the Gemini setting, the guard and rephrase steps use gemini-3.1-flash-lite, and the judge, refine and answer steps use gemini-3.8-flash-high. In the open-weight setting, every step uses Qwen3.5-9B, a 9-billion-parameter model released under the Apache~2.0 licence. It is self-hosted with llama.cpp in 16-bit floating point on two NVIDIA T4 GPUs, with thinking disabled.

\subsection{Evaluation Sets}\label{sec:eval-sets}

The retrieval benchmark must provide the location of the correct answer for every question. We therefore generate 10{,}000 Vietnamese questions from the corpus with rules and templates rather than with a language model. They fall into six groups, with 5{,}000 questions on formulary monographs, 2{,}500 brand-name questions answered by a leaflet, 1{,}000 questions whose answer lies in a single chunk within a long section, and 500 each of naturally phrased patient questions, noisy questions with missing diacritics or look-alike drug names, and multi-intent questions. They cover 17 intents, such as dosage and contraindications, at three difficulty levels, and each is labelled with its gold section. In addition, 1{,}819 questions require a specific chunk or a chunk within a three-chunk window around it, and the 500 multi-intent questions require all of their gold sections. For 1{,}126 formulary questions, the rule-based relevance judgments also accept the leaflet of a product with the same active ingredients.

The end-to-end golden set extends the benchmark with conversational and adversarial cases and is written by the first author together with a pharmacist. It contains 420 answerable questions, 70 drawn from each benchmark group and stratified by difficulty and answer mode, together with 50 multi-turn, 10 unanswerable, 10 out-of-scope and 10 prompt-injection items. Each answerable and multi-turn item has a reference answer and one to five atomic key facts taken from the gold sections. Every key fact includes a verbatim evidence quote, which is automatically verified against the section text. Because the answerable questions reuse benchmark templates, every item is also audited against fixed criteria, for example that the question names its subject, does not leak a document identifier and asks for each of its key facts. Items that fail a check are rewritten, while deliberate noise such as missing diacritics is kept. The unanswerable items concern real drugs whose names do not occur in the corpus. Multi-turn items use a fixed conversation history, so that all configurations receive the same context.

\subsection{Experimental Design}\label{sec:design}

The retrieval experiments compare BM25, six dense models (BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3}, EmbeddingGemma-300M~\cite{vera2025embeddinggemma}, Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B, 4B and 8B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}, and the commercial OpenAI text-embedding-3-large~\cite{openai2024embeddings}), and hybrid RRF of Qwen3-Embedding-4B with BM25 at $k=60$, the common default~\cite{cormack2009rrf}, and at $k=2$. Five rerankers are applied to the top 30 hybrid candidates, namely BGE-reranker-v2-m3~\cite{chen2024bgem3}, the LLM-based BGE-reranker-v2-gemma~\cite{li2024llama2vec} and Qwen3-Reranker 0.6B, 4B and 8B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}. The Qwen3 rerankers use a Vietnamese medical instruction, which raises MRR@30 of the 0.6B model by 0.045 in a pilot on 1{,}000 questions. All runs use the retrieval code of the pipeline on the same index and return 30 chunks per question. A chunk counts as relevant if it belongs to a gold section, lies within the required window for chunk-level questions, or is accepted by the relevance judgments. We report Hit@$k$ for $k\in\{3,5,10,30\}$, MRR@10, MRR@30 and nDCG@10 with binary gains, where each evidence unit, namely the question or, for multi-intent questions, each gold section, counts once at its first relevant chunk. For multi-intent questions we also report the complete-evidence rate (CER@10), the share of questions whose gold sections all appear in the top ten. Cost is described by the embedding dimension and by the highest reranking throughput measured on NVIDIA T4 GPUs.

The end-to-end experiments measure the contribution of retrieval and of the agentic steps with six configurations. Besides the full agent, three ablations each remove one component, namely the rephrase step, the judge--refine loop or the reranker, and two baselines remove several components. All six configurations are run in the Gemini setting. In the open-weight setting, the full agent, one-step RAG and the closed-book baseline are run, so that the gain from retrieval and from the agentic steps can be compared across the two model settings. The closed-book baseline sends the conversation to the answer model with a pharmacist system prompt and no retrieval. One-step RAG applies the guard, a single search with the last user turn as typed and the answer step, which still sees the conversation history, and thus isolates the effect of the agentic steps. An item whose model call fails at any step counts as failed and is run again, so that provider errors cannot appear as shorter successful runs.

The metrics follow the benchmarks reviewed in Sect.~\ref{sec:related}. Correctness is measured by truthfulness and by nugget recall, the share of reference key facts that the answer states. Grounding is measured by faithfulness, the share of claims supported by the evidence given to the model, and by citation recall, the share of content sentences supported by the passages they cite. Behaviour is measured by the response rate on answerable items, negative rejection on unanswerable ones, redirect accuracy on out-of-scope ones, and injection defence, the share of injection items that are blocked or answered without following the injection. Cost is measured by model calls and tokens per turn. A judge labels each key fact as supported, missing or contradicted, and following CRAG~\cite{yang2024cragbench} an answer is \emph{incorrect} if it contradicts a key fact, \emph{missing} if it is not grounded or supports no key fact, \emph{perfect} if it supports all key facts, and \emph{acceptable} otherwise. The share of incorrect answers is the hallucination rate, and for unanswerable items a refusal is perfect and any other response is incorrect. For $N$ items, with $F_j$ the key facts of item $j$ and $S_j\subseteq F_j$ those the answer supports,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:truth}
\mathrm{Truthfulness}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_j,\quad s_j\in\{1,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}},0,-1\},\qquad \mathrm{Recall}_j=\frac{|S_j|}{|F_j|},
\end{equation}
\noindent where $s_j$ scores perfect, acceptable, missing and incorrect answers, so that a wrong answer is penalised more than no answer. For each incorrect answer, the judge also rates the potential harm to a user as none, minor or severe.

All model-based judgments use gemini-3.1-flash-lite with medium reasoning effort, which differs from the answer models of both settings. Each judgment is one small structured decision per key fact, per cited sentence, or per item for harm, refusal and injection, so that a low-cost judge answers one simple question at a time~\cite{zheng2023judge}. To validate the judge, 300 randomly drawn items, 50 per configuration of Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main}, are shuffled, stripped of their configuration and graded blind by a pharmacist with the same rubric. Agreement is reported as Cohen's $\kappa$~\cite{cohen1960kappa} and Gwet's AC1~\cite{gwet2008ac1} for binary labels, and as Spearman's $\rho$ with the mean absolute difference for scores. Prediction-powered inference~\cite{angelopoulos2023ppi,saadfalcon2024ares} corrects the bias of a judge-based mean with the $n$ calibration labels:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ppi}
\hat\mu_{\mathrm{PPI}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{y}_i+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(y_i-\hat{y}_i\right),
\end{equation}
\noindent where $\hat{y}$ is the judge score and $y$ the blind grade.

Rates are reported with Wilson intervals~\cite{wilson1927} and means with percentile bootstrap intervals over 10{,}000 resamples~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}. Each configuration is compared with the full agent on the same items with a paired sign-flip randomization test~\cite{smucker2007significance}, with Holm-adjusted $p$-values across configurations~\cite{holm1979}.
