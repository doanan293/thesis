\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Corpus Construction}\label{sec:corpus}

The corpus combines two public sources. The first is the Vietnamese National Drug Formulary (\textit{Dược thư Quốc gia Việt Nam}, 2nd edition, 2018), a 1{,}668-page PDF with 689 drug monographs and 25 general chapters. The second is a set of 2{,}434 product pages of drug package leaflets collected from a Vietnamese online pharmacy on 24 July 2026, of which 2{,}406 contain a usable instructions-for-use section. Formulary text was extracted with PyMuPDF and its tables with Docling; we repaired broken words and punctuation, merged tables split across pages, removed duplicated tables, and split every monograph into its standard headings while keeping page numbers for citation. Leaflets were converted from HTML to Markdown. An automated audit of the build against the sources reports no errors or warnings for the released corpus; 32 groups of sections with identical regulatory text were reviewed and kept.

Sections are cut into chunks of at most 3{,}000 characters: prose is split at paragraph and sentence boundaries, and tables are split by rows with the header repeated in every chunk. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} summarises the indexed release. Formulary monographs yield many short chunks, whereas leaflets yield fewer, longer ones.

Before embedding, each chunk is enriched deterministically, without any LLM call at indexing time. A context header prepends the document title and heading path (for example \textit{Paracetamol > Liều lượng và cách dùng}); a glossary of 71 abbreviations and technical terms adds a short gloss to the 11{,}031 chunks (44.2\%) that mention them; and 2{,}409 colloquial mappings attach the everyday names patients use for a product (such as \textit{Panadol đỏ}) to all 13{,}790 leaflet chunks. The enrichment therefore costs nothing at query time and is fully reproducible.

\subsection{Agentic RAG Pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the pipeline, implemented with LangGraph. Because the system is meant to run on inexpensive LLMs, each step is a single call with a small structured output, and every control decision---routing, loop limits, budget checks and query de-duplication---is made in code rather than delegated to the model.

\textbf{Guard.} Regular expressions catch common injection and jailbreak patterns; an LLM classifier then labels the turn as a drug question, off-topic, or an attack. Attacks are blocked and off-topic turns are redirected without retrieval.

\textbf{Rephrase.} The turn is rewritten into a standalone query from a rolling conversation summary and the last four turns, which resolves pronouns in follow-up questions; greetings and meta questions are answered without retrieval.

\textbf{Search and rerank.} The first search always uses the standalone query. Qdrant runs a dense search with Qwen3-Embedding-4B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb} and a server-side BM25 search~\cite{robertson2009bm25}, each returning 50 candidates, and fuses them with reciprocal rank fusion~\cite{cormack2009rrf}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrf}
\mathrm{RRF}(d)=\sum_{r\in R}\frac{1}{k+\mathrm{rank}_r(d)},\qquad k=2,
\end{equation}
\noindent keeping the top 30. Qwen3-Reranker-4B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}, served by llama.cpp through its native rerank endpoint, rescores them, and the top 8 are hydrated from PostgreSQL, either as the full section (sections up to 16{,}000 characters) or as the hit chunk with one neighbour on each side.

\textbf{Judge and refine.} The judge decides whether the evidence answers the question and, if not, lists what is missing; refine turns the gaps into one to three new queries that have not been issued before. The loop stops after three search rounds.

\textbf{Answer.} The answer model writes a direct answer with numbered citations; citations that do not point to supplied evidence are removed. The final mode is \emph{grounded}, \emph{partial} (the budget ran out with some evidence) or \emph{abstain} (no usable evidence).

Each turn is bounded by 10 LLM calls, 40{,}000 tokens and 24{,}000 characters of evidence. Models are assigned per role: \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} for guard and rephrase, and \texttt{gemini-3.8-flash-high} for judge, refine and answer, both served through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, so any role can be moved to another provider or a self-hosted model by configuration alone. Retrieval uses exactly the same code path in production and in the benchmark.

\subsection{Evaluation Sets}\label{sec:eval-sets}

\textbf{Retrieval benchmark.} We generated 10{,}000 Vietnamese questions from the corpus with rules and templates, without an LLM, so every question has a known answer location (Table~\ref{tab:eval-sets}). The questions cover 17 intents (general information, adverse reactions, contraindications, pregnancy and lactation, dosage, and others) and three difficulty levels (985 easy, 8{,}447 medium, 568 hard). Each question is labelled with its gold section; 1{,}550 questions additionally require a hit within a three-chunk window around the gold chunk, 269 require the exact chunk, and 500 multi-intent questions require every one of their gold sections. Formulary questions can also be answered by a leaflet of a product with the same active ingredients; rule-based relevance judgments accept such leaflet chunks for 1{,}126 questions.

\textbf{End-to-end golden set.} We drew 420 answerable questions from the benchmark, 70 per group, stratified by difficulty and answer mode, and added 50 multi-turn, 10 unanswerable, 10 out-of-scope and 10 prompt-injection items (Table~\ref{tab:eval-sets}). For every answerable and multi-turn item, a reference answer and one to five atomic key facts (nuggets) were written from the gold sections by an LLM (Claude Opus~5); each key fact carries a verbatim evidence quote that a build step checks against the section text, and items failing the check were rewritten. Unanswerable items name real drugs whose names are verified to be absent from the corpus. Multi-turn items use a fixed conversation history, so an error in an earlier turn cannot propagate and all configurations see the same context. The set is frozen with a SHA-256 digest.

\subsection{Experimental Design}\label{sec:design}

\textbf{Retrieval.} The retrieval study compares BM25, five dense models (BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3}, EmbeddingGemma-300M~\cite{vera2025embeddinggemma} and Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B, 4B and 8B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}), hybrid fusion of Qwen3-Embedding-4B with BM25 at $k=60$ (the common default) and $k=2$, and four rerankers applied to the top 30 hybrid candidates (BGE-reranker-v2-m3, and Qwen3-Reranker 0.6B, 4B and 8B with a Vietnamese medical instruction, which raised MRR@30 of the 0.6B model by 0.0445 (95\% CI [0.031, 0.059]) on a 1{,}000-question pilot). Every run calls the backend retrieval service against the same PostgreSQL and Qdrant release and returns 30 chunks per question. A retrieved chunk is a hit if it belongs to a gold section, or, for chunk-level questions, lies within the required window around the gold chunk; relevance judgments add accepted leaflet chunks. We report Hit@$k$, MRR@10 and nDCG@10 with binary gain, where each evidence unit (one per question, or one per gold section for multi-intent questions) earns gain once, at its first satisfying chunk:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ndcg}
\mathrm{nDCG@}10=\frac{1}{\mathrm{IDCG@}10}\sum_{i=1}^{10}\frac{g_i}{\log_2(i+1)},\qquad g_i\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
\noindent For the 500 multi-intent questions we also report the complete-evidence rate (CER@10), the share of questions whose gold sections all appear in the top 10. Reranker throughput is the best rate measured with llama.cpp on NVIDIA T4 GPUs (one model replica per GPU, or one model split over two GPUs for the 8B reranker).

\textbf{End-to-end configurations.} Table~\ref{tab:e2e-configs} lists the five configurations run on the golden set. One-step RAG is guard, one search with the original question, and answer; it isolates the value of the agentic steps. The three ablations each remove one component from the full agent.

\textbf{End-to-end metrics.} We follow the definitions of established benchmarks. For \emph{correctness}, a judge marks every key fact as supported, missing or contradicted by the answer. Nugget recall is the share of supported key facts~\cite{pradeep2024nuggets}. Following CRAG~\cite{yang2024cragbench}, an answer is \emph{incorrect} if it contradicts any key fact, \emph{missing} if it is not grounded or supports none, \emph{perfect} if it supports all, and \emph{acceptable} otherwise; for unanswerable items a refusal is perfect and anything else incorrect. Truthfulness averages the class scores,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crag}
\mathrm{Truthfulness}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_j,\qquad s_j\in\{1,\ 0.5,\ 0,\ -1\},
\end{equation}
\noindent for perfect, acceptable, missing and incorrect, so a wrong answer costs more than no answer. For every incorrect answer the judge rates the harm a user could suffer by following it as none, minor or severe (for example a wrong dose or a missed contraindication); the severe-harm rate is taken over all grounded answers to answerable and multi-turn items. For \emph{grounding}, faithfulness is the RAGAS share of answer claims supported by the evidence given to the answer model~\cite{es2024ragas}, and citation recall follows ALCE~\cite{gao2023alce}: the share of content sentences whose cited passages support them, with uncited sentences counting as unsupported. For \emph{behaviour}, the response rate is the share of answerable and multi-turn items that receive a grounded answer; negative rejection~\cite{chen2024rgb} is the share of unanswerable items declined, either by the abstain mode or in words; redirect accuracy covers out-of-scope items; and injection defence is the share of injection items that are blocked or answered without following the injected instruction, the complement of the attack success rate~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo}. For \emph{cost} we report LLM calls and tokens per turn; latency is reported for reference only, since it was measured on a development machine with the reranker on a remote GPU.

\textbf{Judging and calibration.} As recommended for LLM-based health studies~\cite{chart2025,gallifant2025tripodllm}, we name every model with its role and settings. All LLM-based judgments use \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} with medium reasoning effort, a model that does not write the answers, through small structured prompts (per key fact, per cited sentence, harm, refusal and injection) so that a low-cost judge makes one simple decision at a time~\cite{zheng2023judge}. To validate the judge, 100 items (50 from the full agent and 50 from one-step RAG, drawn at random) were shuffled, stripped of their configuration and graded blind by \pending{calibration grader}. We report percent agreement, Cohen's $\kappa$ and Gwet's AC1~\cite{gwet2008ac1} for binary labels and Spearman's $\rho$ for scores, each with a bootstrap interval, and treat agreement of at least 0.6 as reliable. For judge-based means we also give prediction-powered estimates that correct the judge's bias with the calibration labels~\cite{angelopoulos2023ppi,saadfalcon2024ares}.

\textbf{Statistics.} Rates carry Wilson intervals~\cite{wilson1927} and means carry percentile bootstrap intervals (10{,}000 resamples, seed 0). Each ablation is compared with the full agent on the same items by a paired sign-flip randomization test~\cite{smucker2007significance}, and the four $p$-values of each metric are adjusted with the Holm procedure~\cite{holm1979}.
