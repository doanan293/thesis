\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

The study consists of four parts. We first build a structured corpus from the two drug-information sources and then design an agentic RAG pipeline on top of it. Two evaluation sets are derived from the corpus, one for retrieval and one for end-to-end question answering, and the experiments on these sets follow a common protocol for metrics and statistical analysis.

\subsection{Corpus Construction}\label{sec:corpus}

The corpus must cover both the reference information used by health professionals and the product-level information that patients ask about. It therefore combines two public sources. The first is the Vietnamese National Drug Formulary (\textit{Dược thư Quốc gia Việt Nam}, 2nd edition, 2018), a 1{,}668-page document with 689 drug monographs and 25 general chapters. The second consists of 2{,}434 product pages with drug package leaflets, collected from a Vietnamese online pharmacy in July 2026, of which 2{,}406 contain a usable instructions-for-use section.

The formulary text was extracted with PyMuPDF and its tables with Docling. Broken words and punctuation were then repaired, tables split across pages were merged, and each monograph was divided into its standard headings with page numbers retained for citation. The leaflets were converted from HTML to Markdown. An automated audit of the final build against the sources reported no errors. Sections are divided into chunks of at most 3{,}000 characters. Prose is split at paragraph and sentence boundaries, and tables are split by rows with the header repeated in each chunk. Table~\ref{tab:corpus} summarises the resulting corpus.

User questions frequently refer to a drug by a colloquial product name or use abbreviations that do not appear in the source text. Each chunk is therefore enriched before embedding with three deterministic additions. A context header prepends the document title and heading path, for example \textit{Paracetamol > Liều lượng và cách dùng}. A glossary of 71 abbreviations and technical terms adds a short explanation to the 11{,}031 chunks that contain them, and 2{,}409 colloquial mappings attach everyday product names, such as \textit{Panadol đỏ}, to the 13{,}790 leaflet chunks. Because no language model is involved, the enrichment is reproducible and adds no cost at query time.

\subsection{Agentic RAG Pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

\input{figures/pipeline}

To avoid depending on the planning ability of low-cost language models, the pipeline in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} fixes the control flow in code and limits every model call to one decision with a small structured output. Routing, loop limits, budget checks and the removal of duplicate queries are handled by the code. The pipeline is implemented with LangGraph and processes a user turn in five steps.

The \emph{guard} step first applies regular expressions for common injection and jailbreak patterns, and then asks the model whether the turn is an attack and whether it concerns medicines. Attacks are blocked, and off-topic turns are redirected without retrieval. The \emph{rephrase} step rewrites the turn into a standalone query, using a rolling conversation summary and the last four turns, so that follow-up questions containing pronouns can be retrieved. The \emph{search} step always starts with this standalone query. It retrieves 50 candidates with dense search and 50 with BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25}, and fuses the two lists with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)~\cite{cormack2009rrf}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrf}
\mathrm{RRF}(d)=\sum_{r\in R}\frac{1}{k+\mathrm{rank}_r(d)},
\end{equation}
\noindent where $R$ is the set of ranked lists and $k$ controls how strongly top ranks are favoured. A cross-encoder reranker rescores the 30 best fused candidates, and the top eight are expanded with their surrounding text before being passed on. The \emph{judge} step then decides whether this evidence answers the question and, if it does not, lists the missing information. The \emph{refine} step converts these gaps into one to three new queries, and the loop ends after at most three search rounds. Finally, the \emph{answer} step writes a direct answer with numbered citations. Citations that do not refer to the supplied evidence are removed, and the answer is marked as grounded, partial or abstained depending on the evidence available.

This design has two advantages over delegating planning to the model. Each model call is a narrow classification or rewriting task, which is better suited to small models. In addition, the per-turn budget of 10 model calls, 40{,}000 tokens and 24{,}000 characters of evidence is enforced regardless of model behaviour, and each step can be disabled for ablation without changing the others.

In our implementation, dense retrieval uses Qwen3-Embedding-4B and reranking uses Qwen3-Reranker-4B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}, both served by llama.cpp. Qdrant stores the dense and BM25 indexes, PostgreSQL stores the corpus, and the fusion constant is $k=2$. The guard and rephrase steps use \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite}, and the judge, refine and answer steps use \texttt{gemini-3.8-flash-high}. Both are low-cost models accessed through an OpenAI-compatible interface, so each role can be moved to another provider or a self-hosted model by configuration.

\subsection{Evaluation Sets}\label{sec:eval-sets}

\input{tables/eval-sets}

The retrieval benchmark must provide the location of the correct answer for every question. We therefore generated 10{,}000 Vietnamese questions from the corpus with rules and templates rather than with a language model (Table~\ref{tab:eval-sets}). The questions cover 17 intents, such as dosage, adverse reactions and contraindications, at three difficulty levels. Each question is labelled with its gold section. In addition, 1{,}819 questions require a specific chunk or a chunk within a three-chunk window around it, and the 500 multi-intent questions require all of their gold sections. Formulary questions can also be answered by the leaflet of a product with the same active ingredients, so rule-based relevance judgments accept such leaflet chunks for 1{,}126 questions.

The end-to-end golden set extends the benchmark with conversational and adversarial cases. It contains 420 answerable questions, 70 drawn from each benchmark group and stratified by difficulty and answer mode, together with 50 multi-turn, 10 unanswerable, 10 out-of-scope and 10 prompt-injection items. For each answerable and multi-turn item, an LLM (Claude Opus~5) wrote a reference answer and one to five atomic key facts from the gold sections. Every key fact includes a verbatim evidence quote, which was automatically verified against the section text, and items that failed this check were rewritten. The unanswerable items concern real drugs whose names do not occur in the corpus. Multi-turn items use a fixed conversation history, so that all configurations receive the same context.

\subsection{Experimental Design}\label{sec:design}

The retrieval experiments compare BM25, five dense models (BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024bgem3}, EmbeddingGemma-300M~\cite{vera2025embeddinggemma} and Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B, 4B and 8B~\cite{zhang2025qwen3emb}), and hybrid RRF of Qwen3-Embedding-4B with BM25 at $k=60$, the common default, and at $k=2$. Four rerankers are applied to the top 30 hybrid candidates: BGE-reranker-v2-m3 and Qwen3-Reranker 0.6B, 4B and 8B. The Qwen3 rerankers use a Vietnamese medical instruction, which improved MRR@30 of the 0.6B model by 0.0445 (95\% CI [0.031, 0.059]) in a pilot on 1{,}000 questions. All runs use the retrieval code of the pipeline on the same index and return 30 chunks per question. A chunk counts as relevant if it belongs to a gold section, lies within the required window for chunk-level questions, or is accepted by the relevance judgments. We report Hit@$k$, MRR@10 and nDCG@10. For nDCG, each evidence unit, namely the question or, for multi-intent questions, each gold section, receives a binary gain once at its first relevant chunk:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ndcg}
\mathrm{nDCG@}10=\frac{1}{\mathrm{IDCG@}10}\sum_{i=1}^{10}\frac{g_i}{\log_2(i+1)},\qquad g_i\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
\noindent For multi-intent questions we also report the complete-evidence rate (CER@10), the share of questions whose gold sections all appear in the top ten. Cost is described by the embedding dimension and by the highest reranking throughput measured on NVIDIA T4 GPUs.

\input{tables/e2e-configs}

The end-to-end experiments measure the contribution of the agentic steps and the safety of the pipeline with the five configurations in Table~\ref{tab:e2e-configs}. One-step RAG applies the guard, a single search with the original question and the answer step, and thus isolates the effect of the agentic steps. Each ablation removes one component from the full agent. Table~\ref{tab:e2e-metrics} lists the metrics, which follow established benchmarks. For correctness, a judge labels each key fact as supported, missing or contradicted. Following CRAG~\cite{yang2024cragbench}, an answer is \emph{incorrect} if it contradicts a key fact, \emph{missing} if it is not grounded or supports no key fact, \emph{perfect} if it supports all key facts, and \emph{acceptable} otherwise. For unanswerable items, a refusal is perfect and any other response is incorrect. Truthfulness is the mean class score,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crag}
\mathrm{Truthfulness}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_j,\qquad s_j\in\{1,\ 0.5,\ 0,\ -1\},
\end{equation}
\noindent for perfect, acceptable, missing and incorrect answers, so that a wrong answer is penalised more than no answer. For each incorrect answer, the judge also rates the potential harm to a user as none, minor or severe. Latency is reported for reference only, because it was measured on a development machine with the reranker on a remote GPU.

\input{tables/e2e-metrics}

All model-based judgments use \texttt{gemini-3.1-flash-lite} with medium reasoning effort, which differs from the model that writes the answers. Each judgment is a small structured decision, made per key fact, per cited sentence, or for harm, refusal and injection, so that a low-cost judge handles one simple question at a time~\cite{zheng2023judge}. To validate the judge, 100 randomly drawn items, 50 from the full agent and 50 from one-step RAG, were shuffled, stripped of their configuration and graded blind by \pending{calibration grader}. Agreement is reported as percent agreement, Cohen's $\kappa$ and Gwet's AC1~\cite{gwet2008ac1} for binary labels and as Spearman's $\rho$ for scores, and values of at least 0.6 are treated as reliable. Prediction-powered estimates use the calibration labels to correct the bias of judge-based means~\cite{angelopoulos2023ppi,saadfalcon2024ares}. Following reporting guidance for LLM-based health studies~\cite{chart2025,gallifant2025tripodllm}, all models are identified together with their roles and settings.

For the statistical analysis, rates are reported with Wilson intervals~\cite{wilson1927} and means with percentile bootstrap intervals based on 10{,}000 resamples. Each ablation is compared with the full agent on the same items using a paired sign-flip randomization test~\cite{smucker2007significance}, and the four $p$-values of each metric are adjusted with the Holm procedure~\cite{holm1979}.
