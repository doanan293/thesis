\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Retrieval-augmented generation conditions a generator on retrieved passages in order to ground knowledge-intensive answers~\cite{lewis2020rag}. Subsequent work lets the system decide when and how to retrieve. Self-RAG trains the generator to emit reflection tokens that trigger retrieval and critique its own output~\cite{asai2024selfrag}. Corrective RAG adds a lightweight evaluator that triggers further retrieval when the retrieved documents are of low quality~\cite{yan2024crag}, and Adaptive-RAG selects no, single-step or multi-step retrieval according to the predicted complexity of the question~\cite{jeong2024adaptiverag}. These methods either require fine-tuning or rely on a capable model for planning. How well such pipelines work when every step is performed by a small, low-cost model has received little attention. Our pipeline targets this setting by keeping the control flow in code.

Retrieval choices have a strong influence on medical RAG. MIRAGE evaluates 41 combinations of corpora, retrievers and language models on 7{,}663 English medical questions and finds that the choice of corpus and retriever has a large effect on accuracy~\cite{xiong2024medrag}. Mikkelsen benchmarks ten embedding models and BM25 on three clinical corpora with deterministic queries, and reports that corpus type, query format and chunking together rival the effect of the model choice~\cite{mikkelsen2026clinical}. Nygren et al.\ answer drug side-effect questions with compact models and report accuracy together with latency~\cite{nygren2026drug}. General benchmarks such as BEIR~\cite{thakur2021beir}, MTEB~\cite{muennighoff2023mteb} and its Vietnamese counterpart VN-MTEB~\cite{pham2025vnmteb} rank embedding models across many tasks. However, none of these benchmarks covers Vietnamese drug labelling, and they rarely report the cost of hybrid fusion and reranking next to their effectiveness. Our benchmark provides both.

The evaluation of RAG systems has converged on a set of complementary automatic measures. RAGAS scores faithfulness and answer relevancy without reference answers~\cite{es2024ragas}, and ARES calibrates LLM judges against a small human-labelled set through prediction-powered inference~\cite{saadfalcon2024ares,angelopoulos2023ppi}. ALCE measures whether each generated statement is supported by the passages it cites~\cite{gao2023alce}. CRAG classifies answers as perfect, acceptable, missing or incorrect so that hallucinations are penalised more than abstentions~\cite{yang2024cragbench}. The TREC 2024 RAG track assesses answers against atomic nuggets~\cite{pradeep2024nuggets}, RGB tests whether models reject questions that the evidence cannot answer~\cite{chen2024rgb}, and AgentDojo measures how often prompt injections succeed against LLM agents~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo}. Reporting guidelines for health chatbots and LLM studies further ask authors to disclose model identifiers, prompting and the assessment procedure~\cite{chart2025,gallifant2025tripodllm}. Each of these works measures one aspect of answer quality, and agentic pipelines are seldom assessed on all of them together. Our evaluation combines these measures with paired ablations and a calibrated judge.

Research on Vietnamese medical question answering has mainly produced datasets for general health information. ViMQ provides patient questions annotated with intents and medical entities~\cite{huy2021vimq}. ViHealthQA contains 10{,}015 health questions answered by experts and was used to study two-stage retrieval with BM25 and Sentence-BERT~\cite{nguyen2022spbertqa}. UIT-ViCoQA targets conversational reading comprehension of health news~\cite{luu2021vicoqa}. Outside the medical domain, URAG combines rule-based and hybrid RAG for a university admission chatbot~\cite{nguyen2025urag}. None of these studies addresses drug labelling or evaluates an agentic RAG system end to end, which is the focus of this paper.
