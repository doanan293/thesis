\subsection{Retrieval}\label{sec:results-retrieval}

\input{figures/retrieval-cost}

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} shows that reranked hybrid retrieval gives the best ranking quality on the benchmark. With Qwen3-Reranker-8B, the hybrid retriever reaches 91.8\% Hit@3, 97.7\% Hit@10, an nDCG@10 of 0.880 and an MRR@10 of 0.855, the highest values in all four columns. Qwen3-Reranker-4B comes within 1.2 points of Hit@3 at 2.7 times the throughput, and raises Hit@3 of the hybrid retriever by 6.3 points. Fig.~\ref{fig:cost} sets these gains against their cost: BGE-reranker-v2-m3 is eight times faster than the 4B model but leaves Hit@10 unchanged, and the last point of Hit@3 costs a 2.7-fold drop in throughput. The pipeline therefore uses the 4B reranker.

Among single retrievers, all dense models outperform BM25 (81.3\% Hit@10), and Qwen3-Embedding-4B matches the 8B model in Hit@10 (95.4\%) with a 1.6 times smaller embedding.

The fusion constant has a large effect on hybrid retrieval. With the common setting $k=60$, the hybrid retriever ranks below the dense retriever alone (nDCG@10 of 0.747 versus 0.820), because the flat weighting promotes candidates that only one retriever ranks highly, whereas $k=2$ gives the highest Hit@10 before reranking (95.9\%).

Complete retrieval of multi-intent questions favours dense retrieval (CER@10 of 98.4\% for Qwen3-Embedding-4B against 91.2\% to 96.4\% for the hybrid and reranked variants), and noisy questions remain the hardest group, with Hit@10 from 54.4\% for BM25 to 82.6\% for Qwen3-Embedding-4B.

\subsection{End-to-End Evaluation}\label{sec:results-e2e}

\input{tables/e2e-main}

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} shows that grounding the model in the corpus is what makes its answers usable. In the API setting, the full agent reaches a truthfulness of 0.891 and a nugget recall of 0.930, against 0.268 and 0.519 for the same answer model without retrieval, and it cuts hallucinated answers from 18.3\% to 1.5\%, a twelvefold reduction. The gap is wider for the open-weight model. Without retrieval, Qwen3.5-9B has a negative truthfulness ($-0.060$), because 30.6\% of its answers are incorrect and only 5.0\% are perfect. Inside the pipeline, the same model reaches a truthfulness of 0.774 and a nugget recall of 0.859, 3.6 times its closed-book value, and severe harm falls from 20.9\% to 1.5\% of the answers.

The full agent is also more accurate than one-step RAG in both settings. With the API models it raises truthfulness from 0.790 to 0.891 and the share of perfect answers from 73.5\% to 83.3\%, and it reduces unanswered questions from 11.1\% to 0.8\%. With the open-weight model the same comparison gives 0.694 against 0.774 in truthfulness and 12.1\% against 2.5\% unanswered questions. All of these differences are significant after the Holm correction, whereas hallucination and severe harm do not differ between the two retrieval-based systems. One-step RAG keeps higher faithfulness and citation recall, probably because its single search returns a smaller and more focused evidence set, and it costs 2.0 LLM calls and 7{,}433 tokens per turn against 5.3 calls and 11{,}621 tokens for the full agent. One API one-step item is excluded because the judge returned an empty response for it in every attempt.

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-ablation} attributes the gain to the rephrase step. Removing it lowers truthfulness by 0.111 and nugget recall by 0.112 and raises the missing rate by 10.2 points, almost exactly the loss of one-step RAG, which drops all agentic steps. Removing the judge--refine loop changes no quality metric significantly, while it saves 3{,}360 tokens and 2.4 LLM calls per turn. Removing the reranker changes no quality metric significantly either.

Fig.~\ref{fig:groups} shows that the effect of rephrasing is concentrated on conversational follow-up questions. On the 50 multi-turn items, one-step RAG leaves 96\% of the questions unanswered with the API models and 88\% with the open-weight model, whereas the full agent answers all of them, with a nugget recall of 0.948 and 0.866. On the 420 single-turn answerable questions the full agent is level with or slightly ahead of one-step RAG (0.928 against 0.909 API, 0.858 against 0.855 open-weight), and it gains most on noisy questions with misspellings or look-alike names (0.934 against 0.845 API). Table~\ref{tab:example} shows a typical multi-turn failure of one-step RAG, whose search runs on the follow-up question as typed.

The automatic judge agrees closely with the blind grader on key facts in both settings ($\kappa$ of 0.848 and 0.864, AC1 of 0.904 and 0.876 over 525 facts each) and on nugget recall ($\rho$ of 0.905 and 0.944). For faithfulness and citation support, rank agreement is weak but the mean absolute differences stay below 0.09, because most scores are close to 1. The calibrated estimates in Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} exceed the judge-based ones for every configuration, so the judge is slightly strict but favours no system.

On the safety cases, the API retrieval-based systems declined all 10 unanswerable questions, redirected all 10 out-of-scope requests and resisted all 10 prompt injections. The open-weight full agent declined all 10, redirected 8 and resisted 9. Neither closed-book baseline declined or redirected a single request, because without a corpus the model has no signal that a drug is unknown to it. With ten items per case, a perfect rate has a 95\% Wilson interval of [0.722, 1.000].

\subsection{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Retrieval, not the size of the language model, is the main source of accuracy: inside the pipeline, a 9-billion-parameter model on two T4 GPUs outperforms the API model without retrieval (0.774 against 0.268 in truthfulness). Among the agentic steps, the value lies in rephrasing, which makes pronoun-based follow-up questions answerable at no cost on single-turn questions, whereas the judge--refine loop adds 2.4 model calls per turn without a significant gain. A practical design therefore keeps the rephrase step and makes further searches conditional. The main weakness of the smaller model is grounding (faithfulness 0.855 against 0.946): the blind grader traced most unsupported claims to answers that mixed in information about a different drug, so a cross-drug attribution check is the most useful next safeguard.
