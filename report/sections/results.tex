\section{Experimental Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Retrieval}\label{sec:results-retrieval}

\input{tables/retrieval}

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} shows that reranked hybrid retrieval gives the best ranking quality on the benchmark. With Qwen3-Reranker-8B, the hybrid retriever reaches 91.8\% Hit@3, 97.7\% Hit@10, an nDCG@10 of 0.880 and an MRR@10 of 0.855, the highest values in all four columns. Qwen3-Reranker-4B comes within 1.2 points of Hit@3 and 0.2 points of Hit@10 at 2.7 times the throughput, and compared with the hybrid retriever alone it raises Hit@3 by 6.3 points and nDCG@10 by 0.075. BGE-reranker-v2-m3 is eight times faster than the 4B model and raises nDCG@10 to 0.837, but it leaves Hit@10 unchanged. The pipeline therefore uses the 4B reranker.

Among single retrievers, all dense models outperform BM25 (81.3\% Hit@10), and Qwen3-Embedding-4B matches the 8B model in Hit@10 (95.4\%) with a 1.6 times smaller embedding.

The fusion constant has a large effect on hybrid retrieval. With the common setting $k=60$, the hybrid retriever ranks below the dense retriever alone (nDCG@10 of 0.747 versus 0.820), because the flat weighting promotes candidates that only one retriever ranks highly. With $k=2$, which favours the top ranks of each list, it achieves the highest Hit@10 before reranking (95.9\%).

Complete retrieval of multi-intent questions favours dense retrieval: Qwen3-Embedding-4B finds all gold sections within the top ten for 98.4\% of these questions, against 91.2\% to 96.4\% for the hybrid and reranked variants. Noisy and confusable questions remain the most difficult group, with Hit@10 ranging from 54.4\% for BM25 to 82.6\% for Qwen3-Embedding-4B.

\subsection{End-to-End Evaluation}\label{sec:results-e2e}

\input{tables/e2e-main}

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} shows that grounding the model in the corpus is what makes its answers usable. In the hosted setting, the full agent reaches a truthfulness of 0.891 and a nugget recall of 0.930, against 0.268 and 0.519 for the same answer model without retrieval, and it cuts hallucinated answers from 18.3\% to 1.5\%, a twelvefold reduction. The gap is wider for the open-weight model. Without retrieval, Qwen3.5-9B has a negative truthfulness ($-0.060$), because 30.6\% of its answers are incorrect and only 5.0\% are perfect. Inside the pipeline, the same model reaches a truthfulness of 0.774 and a nugget recall of 0.859, 3.6 times its closed-book value, and severe harm falls from 20.9\% to 1.5\% of the answers.

The full agent is also more accurate than one-step RAG in both settings. With the hosted models it raises truthfulness from 0.790 to 0.891 and the share of perfect answers from 73.5\% to 83.3\%, and it reduces unanswered questions from 11.1\% to 0.8\%. With the open-weight model the same comparison gives 0.694 against 0.774 in truthfulness and 12.1\% against 2.5\% unanswered questions. All of these differences are significant after the Holm correction, whereas hallucination and severe harm do not differ between the two retrieval-based systems. One-step RAG keeps higher faithfulness and citation recall, probably because its single search returns a smaller and more focused evidence set. The full agent needs 5.3 LLM calls and 11{,}621 tokens per turn against 2.0 calls and 7{,}433 tokens for one-step RAG. One hosted one-step item is excluded because the judge provider returned an empty response for it in every attempt.

\input{tables/e2e-ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-ablation} attributes the gain to the rephrase step. Removing it lowers truthfulness by 0.111 and nugget recall by 0.112 and raises the missing rate by 10.2 points, almost exactly the loss of one-step RAG, which drops all agentic steps. Removing the judge--refine loop changes no quality metric significantly, while it saves 3{,}360 tokens and 2.4 LLM calls per turn. Removing the reranker changes no quality metric significantly either.

The effect of rephrasing is concentrated on conversational follow-up questions. On the 50 multi-turn items, whose follow-up question refers to the drug only by a pronoun, one-step RAG leaves 96\% of the questions unanswered with the hosted models and 88\% with the open-weight model, whereas the full agent answers all of them, with a nugget recall of 0.948 and 0.866. On the 420 single-turn answerable questions the full agent is level with or slightly ahead of one-step RAG (0.928 against 0.909 hosted, 0.858 against 0.855 open-weight), and it gains most on noisy questions with misspellings or look-alike names (0.934 against 0.845 hosted).

The automatic judge agrees closely with the blind calibration grader on key facts in both settings, with Cohen's $\kappa$ of 0.848 and 0.864 and Gwet's AC1 of 0.904 and 0.876 over 525 facts each, and with Spearman's $\rho$ of 0.905 and 0.944 for nugget recall. For faithfulness and supported citation rate, rank agreement is weak but the mean absolute differences stay below 0.09, because most scores are close to 1. The calibrated estimates in Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} are higher than the judge-based ones for every configuration, for example 0.972 against 0.930 for the hosted full agent, which indicates that the judge is slightly stricter than the grader and does not favour any single system.

\subsection{Safety}\label{sec:results-safety}

In the hosted setting, both retrieval-based systems declined all 10 unanswerable questions, redirected all 10 out-of-scope requests and resisted all 10 prompt injections. With the open-weight model, the full agent declined all unanswerable questions, redirected 8 out-of-scope requests and resisted 9 injections. Neither closed-book baseline declined an unanswerable question or redirected an out-of-scope request, because without a corpus the model has no signal that a drug is unknown. With ten items per case, the 95\% Wilson interval of a perfect rate is [0.722, 1.000], so these results exclude only a high failure rate.

\subsection{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The results show that retrieval, not the size of the language model, is the main source of accuracy: inside the pipeline, a 9-billion-parameter model on two T4 GPUs outperforms the hosted model without retrieval by a wide margin (0.774 against 0.268 in truthfulness). Among the agentic steps, the value lies in rephrasing, which makes pronoun-based follow-up questions answerable and costs no accuracy on single-turn questions because the answer step reads both the original and the rewritten question. The judge--refine loop consumed 2.4 additional model calls per turn without a significant gain, so a practical design keeps the rephrase step and searches again only when the evidence is demonstrably insufficient. The main weakness of the smaller model is grounding (faithfulness 0.855 against 0.946): the calibration grader traced most unsupported claims to answers that mixed in information about a different drug, which makes a cross-drug attribution check the most useful next safeguard.
