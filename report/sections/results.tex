\subsection{Retrieval}\label{sec:results-retrieval}

\input{tables/retrieval}

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} shows that reranked hybrid retrieval gives the best ranking quality on the benchmark. With Qwen3-Reranker-4B, the hybrid retriever reaches 90.6\% Hit@3, 97.5\% Hit@10, an nDCG@10 of 0.859 and an MRR@10 of 0.827, the highest values in all four columns. Compared with the hybrid retriever alone, reranking raises Hit@3 by 6.4 points and nDCG@10 by 0.075. The smaller Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B achieves the second-highest Hit@10 (96.8\%) at about three times the throughput. BGE-reranker-v2-m3 is the fastest reranker and raises nDCG@10 to 0.837, but it leaves Hit@10 almost unchanged (95.8\% versus 95.9\%). \pending{8B reranker result}

Among single retrievers, all dense models outperform BM25, whose Hit@10 is 81.3\%. Within the Qwen3 family, effectiveness increases from the 0.6B to the 4B model. The 8B model reaches the same Hit@10 of 95.4\% and a slightly higher nDCG@10 (0.834 versus 0.820) with a 1.6 times larger embedding.

The fusion constant has a large effect on hybrid retrieval. With the common setting $k=60$, the hybrid retriever ranks below the dense retriever alone (nDCG@10 of 0.747 versus 0.820). With $k=2$, which favours the top ranks of each list, it achieves the highest Hit@10 before reranking (95.9\%).

Complete retrieval of multi-intent questions behaves differently from single-answer ranking. The dense Qwen3-Embedding-4B retriever finds all gold sections within the top ten for 98.4\% of these questions, while the hybrid retriever with $k=2$ and its reranked variants reach between 91.2\% and 96.4\%. Noisy and confusable questions remain the most difficult group, with Hit@10 ranging from 54.4\% for BM25 to 82.6\% for Qwen3-Embedding-4B.
