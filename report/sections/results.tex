\subsection{Retrieval}\label{sec:results-retrieval}

\input{figures/retrieval-cost}

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} shows that reranked hybrid retrieval gives the best ranking quality on the benchmark. With Qwen3-Reranker-8B, the hybrid retriever reaches 91.8\% Hit@3, 95.2\% Hit@5, 97.7\% Hit@10, an nDCG@10 of 0.880 and an MRR@10 of 0.855, the highest values in every quality column except CER@10. The hybrid retriever already places a relevant chunk among its 30 candidates for 99.2\% of the questions, so the rerankers act by moving those chunks to the top: Qwen3-Reranker-4B raises Hit@5 by 3.8 points and comes within 0.8 points of the 8B model at 2.7 times the throughput. Fig.~\ref{fig:cost} sets these gains against their cost: BGE-reranker-v2-m3 is eight times faster than the 4B model but gains only 1.4 points of Hit@5 and leaves Hit@10 unchanged, and the last 0.8 points of Hit@5 cost a 2.7-fold drop in throughput. The pipeline therefore uses the 4B reranker.

All dense models outperform BM25, and Qwen3-Embedding-4B matches the 8B model in Hit@10 with a 1.6 times smaller embedding.

The fusion constant $k$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:rrf} matters: at the common setting $k=60$, hybrid retrieval falls below dense retrieval alone, with an nDCG@10 of 0.747 against 0.820, whereas $k=2$ gives the highest Hit@10 before reranking.

Complete retrieval of multi-intent questions favours dense retrieval, and noisy questions remain the hardest group, with Hit@10 from 54.4\% for BM25 to 82.6\% for Qwen3-Embedding-4B.

\subsection{End-to-End Evaluation}\label{sec:results-e2e}

\input{tables/e2e-main}

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} shows that grounding the model in the corpus is what makes its answers usable. In the Gemini setting, the full agent reaches a truthfulness of 0.923 and a nugget recall of 0.946, against 0.268 and 0.519 for the same answer model without retrieval, and it cuts hallucinated answers from 18.3\% to 1.0\%, an eighteenfold reduction. Without retrieval, Qwen3.5-9B even has a negative truthfulness of $-0.060$, because 30.6\% of its answers are incorrect and only 5.0\% are perfect. Inside the pipeline, the same model reaches a truthfulness of 0.913 and a nugget recall of 0.938, nearly four times its closed-book recall, and severe harm falls from 20.9\% to 1.0\% of the answers, while hallucinated answers drop to 1.2\%, a 25-fold reduction.

The full agent is also more accurate than one-step RAG in both settings. With the Gemini models it raises truthfulness from 0.816 to 0.923 and the share of perfect answers from 77.2\% to 88.2\%, and it reduces unanswered questions from 10.5\% to 0.6\%. With the open-weight model the same comparison gives 0.792 against 0.913 in truthfulness and 11.2\% against 0.8\% unanswered questions. All of these differences are significant after the Holm correction, whereas hallucination and severe harm do not differ significantly between the two retrieval-based systems. One-step RAG keeps slightly higher faithfulness and citation recall, probably because its single search returns a smaller evidence set, at 2.0 LLM calls per turn against 5.3 for the full agent. One Gemini one-step item is excluded because the judge returned an empty response for it in every attempt.

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-ablation} attributes the gain to the rephrase step. Removing it lowers truthfulness by 0.114 and nugget recall by 0.110 and raises the missing rate by 10.0 points, almost exactly the loss of one-step RAG, which drops all agentic steps. Removing the judge--refine loop changes no quality metric significantly, while it saves 3{,}360 tokens and 2.4 LLM calls per turn. Removing the reranker changes no quality metric significantly either.

Fig.~\ref{fig:groups} shows that the effect of rephrasing is concentrated on conversational follow-up questions. On the 50 multi-turn items, one-step RAG leaves 96\% of the questions unanswered with the Gemini models and 88\% with the open-weight model, whereas the full agent answers all of them, with a nugget recall of 0.955 and 0.948. On the 420 single-turn answerable questions the full agent is level with or slightly ahead of one-step RAG, with a nugget recall of 0.945 against 0.925 with Gemini and 0.938 against 0.908 with Qwen3.5-9B. It gains most on noisy questions with misspellings or look-alike names, where it reaches 0.942 against 0.865 with Gemini and 0.912 against 0.805 with Qwen3.5-9B. Table~\ref{tab:example} shows a typical multi-turn failure of one-step RAG, whose search runs on the follow-up question as typed.

The automatic judge agrees closely with the pharmacist's blind grades in both settings. On the 525 graded key facts per setting, Cohen's $\kappa$ is 0.848 and 0.864 and Gwet's AC1 is 0.904 and 0.876, and Spearman's $\rho$ on nugget recall is 0.905 and 0.944. For faithfulness and citation support, rank agreement is weak but the mean absolute differences stay below 0.09, because most scores are close to 1. The calibrated estimates in Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} exceed the judge-based ones for every configuration, so the judge is slightly strict but favours no system.

On the safety cases, the Gemini retrieval-based systems declined all 10 unanswerable questions, redirected all 10 out-of-scope requests and resisted all 10 prompt injections. The open-weight full agent declined all 10, redirected 9 and resisted all 10. Neither closed-book baseline declined or redirected a single request, because without a corpus the model has no signal that a drug is unknown to it. With ten items per case, a perfect rate has a 95\% Wilson interval of [0.722, 1.000].

\subsection{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Retrieval, not the size of the language model, is the main source of accuracy: inside the pipeline, a 9-billion-parameter model reaches a truthfulness of 0.913, far above the 0.268 of the Gemini model without retrieval and close to the 0.923 of the Gemini pipeline itself. Among the agentic steps, the value lies in rephrasing, which makes pronoun-based follow-up questions answerable at no cost on single-turn questions, whereas the judge--refine loop adds 2.4 model calls per turn without a significant gain. A practical design therefore keeps the rephrase step and makes further searches conditional. With grounded retrieval, the gap between the open-weight model and the commercial API narrows sharply across all metrics, to 0.952 against 0.965 in faithfulness and 0.695 against 0.724 in citation recall.
