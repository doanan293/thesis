\input{figures/retrieval-cost}

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} shows that reranked hybrid retrieval gives the best ranking quality on the benchmark. With Qwen3-Reranker-8B, the hybrid retriever reaches 91.8\% Hit@3, 95.2\% Hit@5, 97.7\% Hit@10, an nDCG@10 of 0.880 and an MRR@10 of 0.855, the highest values in every quality column except CER@10. The hybrid retriever already places a relevant chunk among its 30 candidates for 99.2\% of the questions, so the rerankers act by moving those chunks to the top. Qwen3-Reranker-4B raises Hit@5 by 3.8 points and comes within 0.8 points of the 8B model at 2.7 times the throughput. Fig.~\ref{fig:cost} sets these gains against their cost. BGE-reranker-v2-m3 is eight times faster than the 4B model but improves Hit@5 by only 1.4 points and leaves Hit@10 unchanged. The LLM-based BGE-reranker-v2-gemma is 3.8 times slower than BGE-reranker-v2-m3 and brings no improvement over the hybrid retriever alone. The pipeline therefore uses the 4B reranker.

All dense models outperform BM25, and Qwen3-Embedding-4B matches the 8B model in Hit@10 with a 2{,}560- rather than 4{,}096-dimensional embedding. Qwen3-Embedding-4B and 8B also outperform the commercial OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, which reaches 95.0\% Hit@10 and an nDCG@10 of 0.800 with a larger 3{,}072-dimensional embedding, so self-hosting the embedding model costs no retrieval quality on this benchmark.

The fusion constant $k$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:rrf} matters, because at the common setting $k=60$ hybrid retrieval falls below dense retrieval alone in nDCG@10, whereas $k=2$ gives the highest Hit@10 before reranking. Multi-intent questions favour dense retrieval in CER@10, and noisy questions remain the hardest group, with Hit@10 from 54.4\% for BM25 to 82.6\% for Qwen3-Embedding-4B.

\subsection{End-to-End Evaluation}\label{sec:results-e2e}

\input{tables/e2e-main}

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} shows that grounding the model in the corpus is what makes its answers usable. In the Gemini setting, the full agent reaches a truthfulness of 0.923 and a nugget recall of 0.946, against 0.268 and 0.519 for the same answer model without retrieval, and it cuts hallucinated answers from 18.3\% to 1.0\%, an eighteenfold reduction. Without retrieval, Qwen3.5-9B even has a negative truthfulness of $-0.060$, because 30.6\% of its answers are incorrect and only 5.0\% are perfect. Inside the pipeline, the same model reaches a truthfulness of 0.913 and a nugget recall of 0.938, severe harm falls from 20.9\% to 1.0\% of the answers, and hallucinated answers drop to 1.2\%, a 25-fold reduction.

The full agent is also more accurate than one-step RAG in both settings. With the Gemini models it raises truthfulness from 0.816 to 0.923 and reduces unanswered questions from 10.5\% to 0.6\%, and with Qwen3.5-9B from 0.792 to 0.913 and from 11.2\% to 0.8\%. These differences are significant after the Holm correction, whereas hallucination and severe harm do not differ between the two retrieval-based systems. One-step RAG keeps slightly higher faithfulness and citation recall, probably because its single search returns a smaller evidence set, at 2.0 LLM calls per turn against 5.3 for the full agent. One Gemini one-step item is excluded because the judge returns no verdict for it.

Table~\ref{tab:e2e-ablation} attributes the performance gains to both query rephrasing and the iterative judge--refine loop. Removing rephrasing lowers truthfulness by 0.114 and nugget recall by 0.110 and raises the missing rate by 10.0 points, which accounts for most of the loss seen in one-step RAG. Removing the judge--refine loop lowers truthfulness by 0.028 and nugget recall by 0.025 while increasing the missing rate by 2.1 points and hallucinated answers by 0.7 points, confirming that evidence verification and iterative refinement successfully recover omitted facts and eliminate unsupported claims, at the cost of 3{,}360 tokens and 2.4 LLM calls per turn. Removing the reranker changes no quality metric significantly.

Fig.~\ref{fig:groups} shows that the effect of rephrasing is concentrated on conversational follow-up questions. On the 50 multi-turn items, one-step RAG leaves 96\% of the questions unanswered with the Gemini models and 88\% with the open-weight model, whereas the full agent answers all of them, with a nugget recall of 0.955 and 0.948. On the 420 single-turn questions the full agent is level with or slightly ahead of one-step RAG, at 0.945 against 0.925 with Gemini and 0.938 against 0.908 with Qwen3.5-9B, and it gains most on noisy questions with misspellings or look-alike names, at 0.942 against 0.865 and 0.912 against 0.805. A typical failure is the follow-up \textit{Vậy thuốc này bảo quản ở nhiệt độ bao nhiêu?} (So at what temperature should this drug be stored?) after a question on phenoxymethylpenicillin. One-step RAG searches the question as typed and lists the storage temperatures of unrelated drugs, whereas the rephrase step restores the drug name and the full agent answers correctly.

The automatic judge agrees closely with the pharmacist's blind grades. On the 525 graded key facts per setting, Cohen's $\kappa$ is 0.848 and 0.864, Gwet's AC1 is 0.904 and 0.876, and Spearman's $\rho$ on nugget recall is 0.905 and 0.944. For faithfulness and citation support, rank agreement is weak but the mean absolute differences stay below 0.09, because most scores are close to 1. The calibrated estimates in Table~\ref{tab:e2e-main} exceed the judge-based ones for every configuration, so the judge is slightly strict but favours no system.

On the safety cases, the Gemini retrieval-based systems decline all 10 unanswerable questions, redirect all 10 out-of-scope requests and resist all 10 prompt injections. The open-weight full agent declines all 10, redirects 9 and resists all 10. Neither closed-book baseline declines or redirects a single request, because without a corpus the model has no signal that a drug is unknown to it. With ten items per case, a perfect rate has a 95\% Wilson interval of [0.722, 1.000].

\subsection{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Grounded retrieval is the main source of accuracy, and the design premise of Sect.~\ref{sec:pipeline} holds. With the control flow in code and one small decision per model call, Qwen3.5-9B runs every step of the pipeline and reaches a truthfulness only 0.010 below that of the commercial Gemini 3.8 Flash, whereas Gemini 3.8 Flash without retrieval stays far below both. Among the agentic steps, rephrasing makes pronoun-based follow-up questions answerable without degrading single-turn questions, while the judge--refine loop recovers omitted facts and suppresses unsupported claims for 2.4 extra LLM calls per turn, so a deployment can keep both modules or make refinement conditional when token budgets are constrained. The gap between the open-weight and the commercial model also narrows on grounding, to 0.952 against 0.965 in faithfulness and 0.695 against 0.724 in citation recall.
